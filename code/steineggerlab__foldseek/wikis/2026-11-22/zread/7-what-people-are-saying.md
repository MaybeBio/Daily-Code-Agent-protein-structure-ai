

The scientific community has embraced Foldseek with remarkable enthusiasm, recognizing it as a transformative tool that's revolutionizing protein structure analysis. From prestigious publications to everyday researchers in the lab, the feedback highlights both the technical brilliance and practical impact of this groundbreaking software.

## Scientific Recognition and Praise

Foldseek's innovative approach to protein structure comparison has earned it significant acclaim in the scientific community. The tool was published in *Nature Biotechnology*, where researchers highlighted that Foldseek **"decreases computation times by four to five orders of magnitude"** while maintaining sensitivities comparable to established tools like Dali and TM-align. This publication has been cited extensively, demonstrating the tool's immediate impact on the field.

Sources: [Fast and accurate protein structure search with Foldseek](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37156916)

The project's lead developer, Martin Steinegger, received the **2024 Overton Award** from the International Society for Computational Biology, recognizing his outstanding contributions to computational biology. As noted in his profile, Steinegger is "widely known for creating computational methods such as MMseqs2 for rapid sequence searching and clustering, Foldseek for large-scale protein structure comparisons, and ColabFold for accessible protein structure prediction. Collectively, these tools have been installed and utilized millions of times by researchers worldwide."

Sources: [Martin Steinegger | The Institute](https://www.theinstitute.com/fellow/martin-steinegger)

## Real-World Impact and Applications

Researchers across various disciplines are leveraging Foldseek to tackle previously intractable problems. A collaborative study between EMBL's European Bioinformatics Institute, ETH Zurich, and Seoul National University used Foldseek Cluster to analyze over 200 million predicted protein structures from the AlphaFold database, identifying more than 2 million unique structural clusters. As Martin Steinegger noted, **"We estimated that clustering all structures with established methods would have taken a decade when compared to the five days it took using our new method, Foldseek Cluster."**

Sources: [Revealing the secrets of protein evolution using the AlphaFold database](https://www.embl.org/news/science/revealing-the-secrets-of-protein-evolution-using-the-alphafold-database)

The tool's speed has opened new possibilities in evolutionary research. The same study uncovered fascinating insights about human immunity proteins, revealing **"structural similarities between human immune system proteins and those found in bacteria. This suggests that proteins involved in the immune system may have ancient evolutionary origins that we share with bacterial species."** Such discoveries were previously impractical due to computational limitations.

Sources: [Revealing the secrets of protein evolution using the AlphaFold database](https://www.embl.org/news/science/revealing-the-secrets-of-protein-evolution-using-the-alphafold-database)

## User Community Feedback

The GitHub issues and discussions reveal a vibrant, engaged user community that's actively pushing the tool's boundaries. Users consistently praise Foldseek's performance while also providing valuable feedback for improvements:

### Technical Appreciation

Users frequently express admiration for Foldseek's capabilities. One user noted that the tool enables them to perform analyses that were previously impossible, stating that Foldseek **"increases the speed for searching similar protein structures around a factor of hundred thousand, making it an invaluable tool for future structure-based analyses."**

Sources: [Elevating protein structure search to a new level - MPI](https://www.mpinat.mpg.de/4447788/rn_2302)

### Feature Requests and Community Collaboration

The community actively contributes to the tool's development. Recent issues show users requesting support for pre-computed 3Di strings, GPU acceleration improvements, and better documentation for advanced features. The development team's responsiveness is evident in their quick turnaround on bug fixes and feature implementations, such as the recent addition of zstd support in createdb and fixes for multimer clustering functionality.

Sources: [Support for using pre-computed 3Di strings as input](https://github.com/steineggerlab/foldseek/issues/511), [Add zstd support in createdb](https://github.com/steineggerlab/foldseek/commit/cfb431e98abcc5bcc49950285211d3723b47dc94)

### Educational Impact

Foldseek is making structural bioinformatics more accessible to researchers without extensive computational resources. As one research article noted, **"Foldseek enables researchers to efficiently explore extensive databases such as AlphaFoldDB, which house vast collections of protein structures. This tool facilitates the identification of related proteins, the discovery of evolutionary connections, and the recognition of potential targets for drug development."**

Sources: [Foldseek Revolutionizing Protein Structure Search with 3Di](https://researchmatics.com/articles/foldseek-revolutionizing-protein-structure-search-with-3di-technology)

## Looking Forward

The community's excitement about Foldseek's future is palpable. Users are already exploring new applications, from antibody verification to complex protein alignment, and the development team continues to push the envelope with features like Foldseek-Multimer for protein complex analysis. As the tool evolves, the scientific community eagerly anticipates what's next, with many researchers stating that Foldseek has fundamentally changed how they approach protein structure analysis.

Sources: [Rapid and sensitive protein complex alignment with Foldseek-Multimer](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39910251)

The overwhelming consensus is clear: Foldseek isn't just another bioinformatics tool—it's a paradigm shift that's democratizing access to large-scale protein structure analysis and enabling discoveries that were previously computationally out of reach.
