### Classical Pair Potentials
Classical pair potentials, such as Lennard-Jones, Morse, and Soft Sphere, are fundamental models for describing interatomic interactions in atomistic simulations. Within TorchSim, these potentials are implemented through generic frameworks like [`PairPotentialModel`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/pair_potential.py#L277) and [`PairForcesModel`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/pair_potential.py#L467), which are designed to handle batched systems, periodic boundary conditions, and efficient neighbor list generation. These models adhere to the common [`ModelInterface`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/interface.py#L71) for energy, force, and stress calculations, enabling their integration into various simulation workflows. See [Model Interface and Composition](#model-implementations-and-integration-model-interface-and-composition) for more details.

The [`PairPotentialModel`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/pair_potential.py#L277), defined in [`/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/pair_potential.py`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/pair_potential.py), is used for potentials that can be expressed as a scalar energy function of pairwise distances. This model automatically derives forces and virial stresses by leveraging PyTorch's automatic differentiation capabilities. For instance, the [`LennardJonesModel`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/lennard_jones.py#L74) in [`/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/lennard_jones.py`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/lennard_jones.py) and [`MorseModel`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/morse.py#L78) in [`/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/morse.py`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/morse.py) instantiate [`PairPotentialModel`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/pair_potential.py#L277) by providing specific energy functions like [`lennard_jones_pair`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/lennard_jones.py#L23) and [`morse_pair`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/morse.py#L23), respectively. The model efficiently accumulates forces and virial stresses across all interacting pairs, optionally reducing computations for symmetric interactions.

In contrast, the [`PairForcesModel`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/pair_potential.py#L467), also in [`/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/pair_potential.py`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/pair_potential.py), is utilized when interactions are directly defined by a force magnitude function, without an explicit underlying energy potential. This is particularly useful for modeling non-conservative or asymmetric interactions. The [`ParticleLifeModel`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/particle_life.py#L57) in [`/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/particle_life.py`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/particle_life.py) is an example, where [`particle_life_pair_force`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/particle_life.py#L24) directly calculates force magnitudes. Since there's no energy function, this model explicitly returns zero energies while still computing forces and virial stresses.

Both [`PairPotentialModel`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/pair_potential.py#L277) and [`PairForcesModel`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/pair_potential.py#L467) manage neighbor list generation using a configurable function, typically [`torchsim_nl`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/neighbors/__init__.py#L46), to identify interacting particles within a specified cutoff distance. This ensures that interactions are only computed between relevant atomic pairs, contributing to computational efficiency. They also include robust mechanisms for calculating virial stress, which accounts for the contribution of interatomic forces to the system's stress tensor. The [`SoftSphereModel`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/soft_sphere.py#L175) and [`SoftSphereMultiModel`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/soft_sphere.py#L248) in [`/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/soft_sphere.py`](https://github.com/torchsim/torch-sim/torch_sim/models/soft_sphere.py) further extend these concepts, providing repulsive soft-sphere potentials for single and multiple atomic species, demonstrating how the generic frameworks can be specialized for different physical interactions.
