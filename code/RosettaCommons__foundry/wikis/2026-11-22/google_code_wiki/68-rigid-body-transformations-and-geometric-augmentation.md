### Rigid Body Transformations and Geometric Augmentation
Foundry's deep learning infrastructure incorporates utilities for handling 3D rigid transformations, which are fundamental in structural bioinformatics models. These utilities, primarily found in [`/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/utils/rigid.py`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/utils/rigid.py), abstract complex geometric operations, such as rotations and translations, for efficient manipulation of molecular coordinates within a deep learning framework.

The [`Rotation`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/utils/rigid.py#L278) class within [`/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/utils/rigid.py`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/utils/rigid.py) encapsulates 3D rotations, supporting both 3x3 rotation matrices and 4-element quaternions. It provides methods for composing rotations, applying them to 3D points, and inverting them. Similarly, the [`Rigid`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/utils/rigid.py#L850) class, also in [`/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/utils/rigid.py`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/utils/rigid.py), combines a [`Rotation`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/utils/rigid.py#L278) object with a 3D translation vector to represent complete rigid transformations. This class supports composition with other rigid transformations, application to points, and inversion. Both classes offer conversions to and from common tensor representations, such as 4x4 homogeneous matrices or 7-element quaternion+translation vectors. A notable feature is the ability to construct a [`Rigid`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/utils/rigid.py#L850) transformation from three 3D points, which is used for defining local coordinate frames. These utilities are designed with robustness in mind, particularly for use with Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP) training, by implementing core matrix and vector multiplications to avoid potential numerical issues.

Complementing these rigid body transformations are geometric augmentation methods, located in [`/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/utils/rotation_augmentation.py`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/utils/rotation_augmentation.py). These methods are used to introduce variability into training data by applying random rotations and translations to batches of atomic coordinates. This augmentation helps improve the generalization of models by exposing them to diverse orientations of molecular structures. Key functions include [`uniform_random_rotation`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/utils/rotation_augmentation.py#L37) for generating random rotation matrices and [`centre_random_augmentation`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/utils/rotation_augmentation.py#L30) which first centers atom coordinates and then applies random rotations and translations. This process is crucial for training models that are invariant to the global orientation and position of molecular complexes. For further details on how these utilities integrate into the broader training system, refer to [Training Harness and Distributed Training](#training-harness-and-distributed-training).
