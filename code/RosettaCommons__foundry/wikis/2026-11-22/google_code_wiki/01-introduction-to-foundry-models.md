## Introduction to Foundry Models
The Foundry framework provides a collection of biomolecular design models, each tailored for specific tasks in protein, ligand, and nucleic acid engineering. These models address key challenges in structural biology, such as inverse folding, *de novo* design, and protein folding prediction.

For inverse folding and sequence design, the framework includes re-implementations of the widely used ProteinMPNN, LigandMPNN, and SolubleMPNN models. These models are designed to generate amino acid sequences for a given protein backbone structure, or to design sequences that bind to specific ligands, respectively. The core functionality of these models involves graph featurization and message passing layers to process structural information and predict optimal sequences. Further details on the architecture and functionality can be found in [Protein and Ligand Inverse Folding (MPNN) Architecture and Core Functionality](#introduction-to-foundry-models-protein-and-ligand-inverse-folding-mpnn-architecture-and-core-functionality). Data preparation and training infrastructure are described in [MPNN Data Preparation and Training Pipelines](#introduction-to-foundry-models-mpnn-data-preparation-and-training-pipelines), while inference and evaluation metrics are covered in [MPNN Inference and Evaluation](#introduction-to-foundry-models-mpnn-inference-and-evaluation).

For protein folding prediction, Foundry incorporates RoseTTAFold3 (RF3), which predicts the three-dimensional structure of proteins from their amino acid sequences. This model integrates advanced structural components and a diffusion module to refine predictions, and it can include confidence estimation for the predicted structures. The intricate architecture of RF3, including its confidence estimation capabilities, is explored in [RF3 Model Architecture and Confidence Estimation](#introduction-to-foundry-models-rf3-model-architecture-and-confidence-estimation). Advanced data processing, kinematic calculations, and diverse input handling are detailed in [RF3 Advanced Data Processing and Kinematics](#introduction-to-foundry-models-rf3-advanced-data-processing-and-kinematics), and the loss functions and evaluation metrics guiding its training are outlined in [RF3 Loss Functions and Evaluation Metrics](#introduction-to-foundry-models-rf3-loss-functions-and-evaluation-metrics).

The framework also features RFdiffusion3 (RFD3), a generative diffusion model for *de novo* biomolecular interaction design. RFD3 excels at creating novel proteins, nucleic acids, and small molecules with desired binding properties. It employs an iterative denoising process to generate designs, and its [`InputSpecification`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/docs/input.md#L112) allows for precise control over design constraints, such as defining specific contig regions or ligand interactions. The diffusion process and input specification are further discussed in [RFD3 Diffusion Process and Input Specification](#introduction-to-foundry-models-rfd3-diffusion-process-and-input-specification), and its training and advanced conditioning strategies are described in [RFD3 Training and Advanced Conditioning](#introduction-to-foundry-models-rfd3-training-and-advanced-conditioning).

An extension of RFD3, RFdiffusion3NA (RFD3NA), provides comprehensive capabilities for multipolymer design, handling complex protein-DNA-RNA systems. This model offers specialized [`contig`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/src/rfd3/inference/input_parsing.py#L134) specifications for nucleic acids and advanced [`InputSelection`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/src/rfd3/inference/parsing.py#L26) for detailed control over residue and atom selections, including secondary structure and base-pairing constraints for nucleic acid designs. The multipolymer [`contig`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/src/rfd3/inference/input_parsing.py#L134) specification and input selection are detailed in [RFD3NA Multipolymer Contig Specification and Input Selection](#introduction-to-foundry-models-rfd3na-multipolymer-contig-specification-and-input-selection). Extensive data transformations and symmetry operations essential for maintaining structural integrity in multipolymer design are elaborated in [RFD3NA Data Transforms and Symmetry Operations](#introduction-to-foundry-models-rfd3na-data-transforms-and-symmetry-operations).

These models are integrated into the broader Foundry ecosystem, leveraging shared utilities for configuration, training, and inference. The ecosystem provides a robust foundation for biomolecular structure manipulation and processing, enabling researchers and engineers to develop and apply these advanced models effectively.
