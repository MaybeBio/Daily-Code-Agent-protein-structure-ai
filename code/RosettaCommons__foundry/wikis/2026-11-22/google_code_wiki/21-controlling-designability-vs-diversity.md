#### Controlling Designability vs. Diversity
RFdiffusion3 (RFD3) offers mechanisms to fine-tune the balance between generating highly designable (i.e., structurally stable and functional) biomolecules and exploring a broader range of diverse (i.e., varied structural motifs) designs. This trade-off is critical for various protein design applications, where either highly optimized solutions or novel scaffolds may be prioritized. The primary controls for this balance are implemented through inference parameters and input specifications.

One method for adjusting this balance is through **low-temperature sampling**, controlled by parameters in the inference sampler. The [`inference_sampler.step_scale`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/docs/input.md#L69) parameter, with a default of [`1.5`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/docs/input.md#L69), influences the step size during the diffusion process. Increasing this value (e.g., to [`3`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/README.md#L93)) pushes the model towards more deterministic paths, resulting in higher designability but reduced diversity in the generated outputs. Conversely, decreasing it allows for greater exploration of the latent space, increasing diversity. Similarly, [`inference_sampler.gamma_0`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/docs/designability_vs_diversity.md#L8), which defaults to [`0.6`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/pyproject.toml#L61), modulates the amount of noise introduced at each denoising step. Lowering [`gamma_0`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/src/rfd3/model/inference_sampler.py#L37) (e.g., to [`0.2`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/pyproject.toml#L33)) reduces randomness, favoring more probable and thus potentially more designable structures, at the expense of diversity. These settings are applied via the command-line interface during inference, as detailed in the RFD3 documentation, particularly in [`/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/docs/designability_vs_diversity.md`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/docs/designability_vs_diversity.md) and [RFD3 Input Specification and Conditioning](#rfdiffusion3-rfd3-for-de-novo-design-rfd3-input-specification-and-conditioning). Practical examples of how these parameters affect design outcomes, especially in protein-protein interface design, are provided in documents like [`/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/docs/examples/protein_binder_design.md`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/docs/examples/protein_binder_design.md).

Another key control for influencing designability versus diversity lies in the [`is_non_loopy`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/src/rfd3/inference/input_parsing.py#L191) setting within the input JSON/YAML configuration. When [`is_non_loopy`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/src/rfd3/inference/input_parsing.py#L191) is set to [`True`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/examples/all.ipynb#L175), the model is encouraged to generate structures with more defined secondary structure elements and fewer unstructured loop regions. This tends to increase the designability of the generated proteins, as structured proteins are often more stable and predictable. However, this preference for structured designs can lead to a slight reduction in the diversity of novel structural motifs explored. This setting is particularly relevant in tutorials such as [`/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/docs/tutorials/binder_design_tutorial.md`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/docs/tutorials/binder_design_tutorial.md) and [`/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/docs/examples/protein_binder_design.md`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/docs/examples/protein_binder_design.md), where the creation of robust protein binders is often a primary goal. The document [`/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/docs/designability_vs_diversity.md`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/models/rfd3/docs/designability_vs_diversity.md) offers visual examples illustrating the impact of these settings on design output characteristics, including metrics related to refolding pass rates and structural clustering.
