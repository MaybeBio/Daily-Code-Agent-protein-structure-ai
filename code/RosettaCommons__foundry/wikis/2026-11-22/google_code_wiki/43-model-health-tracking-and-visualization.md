### Model Health Tracking and Visualization
Foundry employs the [`ActivationsGradientsWeightsTracker`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/callbacks/health_logging.py#L48) callback, defined in [`/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/callbacks/health_logging.py`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/callbacks/health_logging.py), to monitor the health of neural network models during training. This callback is a specialized implementation of the [Core Callback Design and Extension](#machine-learning-callbacks-core-callback-design-and-extension) and tracks key statistics related to a model's activations, gradients, and weights. It is particularly useful for identifying issues such as vanishing or exploding gradients, or dead neurons, which can impede model training.

The [`ActivationsGradientsWeightsTracker`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/callbacks/health_logging.py#L48) operates by integrating into the training loop, utilizing various hooks to collect data. During training batches, it registers forward hooks on specified modules to capture activation data. Before each optimizer step, it gathers statistics from model parameters, including gradients and weights. This information can be collected as scalar metrics (e.g., mean, standard deviation, norm, min, max) or as histogram data, providing a detailed view of the distributions. The callback offers flexible filtering mechanisms, allowing users to specify which parameters or modules to track, reducing computational overhead and focusing on relevant components. All logging operations are designed to function correctly within distributed training environments by ensuring metrics are collected and reported only by the main process. Additionally, the module provides utility functions, [`plot_tensor_hist`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/callbacks/health_logging.py#L258) and [`plot_tensor_stats`](https://github.com/rosettacommons/foundry/src/foundry/callbacks/health_logging.py#L324), for visualizing the collected time-series and histogram data, enabling a quick assessment of how these critical health indicators evolve over the training process.
